Social Media Strategy Starts Before You Create Content

When a business asks for social media marketing, the first question shouldn't be "What should we post?". Here is the 6-pillar framework that builds real commercial strategy before opening Canva or CapCut.

Rupam Das Social Media Marketer • Meta Ads Specialist

Executive Summary & Key Takeaways

  • Content is not the strategy: Content is merely one of the vehicles used to execute an underlying business strategy.
  • The Iceberg Metaphor: What users see in their feed (posts, reels, stories) is just the 10% tip of the iceberg. The 90% underwater is the foundation: business context, goals, KPIs, audience psychographics, and customer journey.
  • 6 Connected Areas: Strategy connects Business Description → SMART Goals → Actionable KPIs → Target Audience → Customer Journey → Platform Selection.

Figure 1: The Social Media Strategy Iceberg — Content is what people see, but deep strategic alignment is what drives sustainable ROI.

Before deciding what creative to design or what copy to write, I need to understand the business, its current situation, what it wants to achieve, who it needs to reach, how those people move through the buying journey, and which platforms make genuine financial sense for that business.

That is why I build my Social Media Strategy around six connected areas:

01

Business Description & Context

First, I analyze the business in its actual commercial context. The same social media approach cannot work for every business. A new business, an established brand, a B2B SaaS company, a local service business, and an e-commerce fashion brand have completely different unit economics and sales cycles.

Key questions I analyze:

  • What does the business offer, and what is its core value proposition?
  • What products or services provide the highest profit margins?
  • What is the current market situation, and what bottlenecks exist?
02

Setting a SMART Goal

After understanding the business, I define what we want to achieve. Social media without specific goals becomes random activity without direction.

Specific: Clear outcome
Measurable: Trackable metrics
Achievable: Realistic scope
Relevant: Moves the business
Time-bound: Concrete deadline
Vague Goal:

â€œWe want to grow on social media and get more followers.â€

SMART Strategic Goal:

â€œGenerate 50 qualified leads through Meta ads and organic funnels within 90 days at a CPA under $15.â€

03

Defining Actionable KPIs

A goal tells us where we want to go. A KPI (Key Performance Indicator) helps us understand whether we are moving in the right direction.

I don't choose KPIs just because they are easy to track or look nice in vanity screenshots. Every KPI must have a direct mathematical relationship with the overarching business goal:

Qualified Leads Direct Sales Conversion Rate CTR (Click-Through Rate) ROAS & ROI Targeted Reach
04

Target Audience Psychographics

Target audience goes far beyond basic demographics like age, gender, or city. Social media is not about talking to everyone — it is about reaching the right people with the right message.

Pain Points

What specific daily problems keep them up at night?

Objections

What hesitations prevent them from purchasing?

Motivations

What desired transformation are they looking for?

06

Choosing the Right Social Media Platform

Many businesses fall into the trap of thinking they need to be everywhere at once: Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, TikTok, YouTube, Pinterest, X, and Threads.

Being everywhere dilutes your resources. Instead, we choose platforms based on a simple strategic equation:

Platform Selection = Business Model + Target Audience + Customer Journey + Content Format
  • B2B / SaaS / Professional Services: LinkedIn is paramount for reaching corporate decision-makers and founders.
  • In-Depth Educational Content: YouTube is ideal for searchable, long-shelf-life video breakdowns.
  • E-commerce & Visual Lifestyle: Instagram, Pinterest, and TikTok drive visual discovery and high-intent impulse shopping.

These Six Areas Together Form the Strategy

I do not consider Business Description, Goal, KPI, Audience, Customer Journey, and Platform Selection as separate checklists. They form a single interconnected foundation.
